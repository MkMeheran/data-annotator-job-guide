# Data Annotator Guide: What the Job Is, How to Prepare, and My Interview Notes

I am a student who is trying to get work as a data annotator. I keep my notes in this repository: what the job is, how to prepare for it, and what happened in each test or interview I go through. I add a new folder every time I try a new company.

These notes are not official. I do not share real test questions or answers. I only share the type of tasks and what I learned.

## What is a data annotator?

A data annotator labels, checks or rates data so that AI models can learn from it. Models learn from examples, so the examples must be correct. The annotator is the person who makes them correct.

## What the work looks like

- Labeling text: names of people and places, sentiment, topic
- Drawing boxes or outlines around objects in images
- Comparing two images or two AI answers and choosing the better one
- Transcribing or checking audio
- Fact-checking and flagging unsafe or low-quality content
- Reviewing the work of other annotators

## What companies look for

- Attention to detail
- Following written guidelines exactly
- Consistency across hundreds of tasks
- Clear written English, or the language of the project
- Honesty: saying "I am not sure" instead of guessing
- Basic knowledge of the topic, for example medicine, law or code for expert projects

## How to prepare

1. Read the project guidelines slowly and more than once. Most mistakes come from a rule that was not read carefully.
2. Practice careful, boring tasks: counting words, comparing strings, spotting small differences in images.
3. Improve your short writing. Many tests ask for a summary or an explanation, so practice writing clearly in a small word limit.
4. Try a free annotation tool such as Label Studio or CVAT, so the idea of labeling is not new to you.
5. Prepare to talk about your own mistakes. Interviewers like people who can say what went wrong and what they changed.
6. Prepare your setup: stable internet, a quiet place and enough time without interruptions.

## Be careful with scams

- A real job never asks you to pay money to get hired.
- Check the company name and its website before you share documents.
- Read how and when you will be paid before you start.
